== 0. TL;DR

- What: point the iPad at a Thai medicine envelope (ซองยา). The app shows huge, simple text (what the medicine is, how many, and when),
  reads it aloud in Thai, and adds it to today's medicine schedule.
- Who: older people (ผู้สูงอายุ) who take several medicines and can't read the tiny print, and the grandchildren who worry about them.
- AI: Apple's Vision reads the text on the iPad (a neural network on the Neural Engine). A Thai label reader turns that text into
  "1 tablet, morning + evening, after meals". An LLM (on-device Foundation Models if it supports Thai, otherwise the event's AI cluster) double-checks messy labels.
- ⚠️ The #1 thing to do tonight: a 30-minute test on the iPad (section 6.2). As of iOS 26.1, Apple's on-device model does not list Thai
  as a supported language. iPadOS 27 might be different, but nobody knows yet. That test decides Plan A or Plan B.
- Demo: airplane mode on → scan a fake envelope → big card + Thai voice → add to today → tick "taken". 2 minutes, no internet.
- Feature freeze: Day 2, 17:00. After that, only fix bugs, rehearse, and make the deck.

   🧪 Spike test results so far (iPad Pro 11" simulator, iOS 27.0, 2026-10-17 16:35)
| Test | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Vision reads Thai | ✅ `th` is supported | The camera → text part works on-device |
| 2. On-device AI available | ✅ (simulator on the M-chip Mac) | Check again on the real iPad |
| 3. On-device AI supports Thai | ❌ `supportsLocale(th_TH) = false`. Languages: da, de, en, es, fr, it, ja, ko, nb, nl, pt, sv, tr, vi, zh | Thai still isn't supported in iOS 27.0 |
| 4. On-device AI reads a Thai label | ❌ error "May contain sensitive content" (0.8 s) | Apple's safety guardrails blocked the medicine text. Possible fix to try: `SystemLanguageModel(guardrails: .permissiveContentTransformations)` (iOS 26 API) |
| 5. Thai voice | ✅ Kanya (th-TH) | Read-aloud works offline |
| 6. Thai rules | ✅ 5/5 labels correct | The on-device core works |
| 8. Private Cloud Compute | ⚠️ available, but needs the `com.apple.developer.private-cloud-compute` entitlement. Without it the app crashes (fatal error, not catchable) | It's a project-settings change, so it needs team approval and may not work with a free Personal Team |

➡️ So far it's Plan B: Vision + Thai rules on-device, and the AI cluster for the LLM double-check. Confirm on the real iPad.

⚠️ Gotcha found: `AVSpeechSynthesisVoice.speechVoices()` freezes the app if called on the main thread. Call it from `Task.detached { … }`.
(`AVSpeechSynthesizer.speak` with `AVSpeechSynthesisVoice(language: "th-TH")` was fine on the main thread.)

== 1. The idea in one sentence

  | "ยาใจ reads grandma's medicine envelopes out loud, in big clear Thai, so she takes the right pill at the right time, even with no internet."

Name idea: ยาใจ (YaJai). ยา = medicine, ใจ = heart, and ยาใจ is also a Thai word for a beloved person ("sweetheart / the apple of my eye").
Pitch line: *"It's like having your grandchild next to you, reading your medicine for you."* (Other names: อ่านยา, PillPal, ซองยาพูดได้.)

== 2. The problem, with real facts

   2.1 The story (fill in with YOUR real person)
  | "My grandma takes [N] medicines every day. Each one comes in a small plastic envelope from the hospital with a tiny printed sticker.
  | The drug name is in English. The instructions are small Thai text. They all look the same.
  | Last month she [took the evening pill in the morning / forgot one / took two]."

Replace the brackets with what your real person says in the interview (section 3). A real story beats any statistic.

   2.2 Facts you can say (each is checked, with a source in section 18)

| Fact | How sure | Say it like this |
|---|---|---|
| About 1 in 5 Thai people is 60 or older. Thailand is an "aged society". | ✅ Sources range from ~18% (13.6 M people, GISTDA) to ~20% (~15 M, Labour Ministry 2026; 20.17% in 2023 per NESDC/Statista) | "About one in five Thais is over 60. That's around 13–15 million people." |
| WHO estimates medication errors cost about US$42 billion a year worldwide (~1% of global health spending). | ✅ WHO "Medication Without Harm" (2017), repeated in 2022 | "The WHO says medicine mistakes cost the world about 42 billion dollars a year." |
| WHO's goal: cut severe, avoidable medication harm by 50%. | ✅ Same campaign | Good for the "why it matters" slide |
| Polypharmacy means taking 5 or more medicines at once. It's common in older people. | ✅ Standard definition | "Many older people take 5 or more medicines. Doctors call this polypharmacy." |
| Almost everyone over ~40–45 gets presbyopia (ภาวะสายตายาวตามอายุ): small print up close gets blurry. | ✅ General medical knowledge | "After 40, almost everyone finds small print harder to read." |
| Thai health data is "sensitive data" under the PDPA (พ.ร.บ.คุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล พ.ศ. 2562, fully in force since 1 June 2022). | ✅ | "Medicine info is sensitive data under the PDPA, so we keep it on the device." |

  | ❌ Don't invent numbers like "30% of grandmas take the wrong pill." Judges can tell.
  | Use the facts above plus your own test numbers (section 3.3). Those are the strongest of all.

   2.3 Why it happens (the root causes)
1. Tiny text on a sticker, and older eyes (presbyopia).
2. The drug name is English or Latin (e.g. *Amlodipine*). Many older Thais can't read or remember it.
3. Every envelope looks the same.
4. Many medicines with different times (morning / noon / evening / bedtime, before or after meals).
5. Family isn't home during the day to help.

== 3. Check that the problem is real: the interview

Do this tonight by phone or video call with a grandparent, an older neighbor, or a parent who looks after one. 15 minutes.

   3.1 Questions (Thai first, English below)
1. ตอนนี้ทานยากี่ชนิดคะ/ครับ? *(How many medicines do you take?)*
2. รู้ได้ยังไงว่ายาไหนต้องกินตอนไหน? *(How do you know which one to take when?)*
3. อ่านตัวหนังสือบนซองยาได้ชัดไหม? *(Can you read the envelope text clearly?)*
4. เคยกินยาผิด ลืมกิน หรือกินซ้ำไหม? เล่าให้ฟังหน่อย *(Have you ever taken the wrong one, forgotten, or taken one twice? Tell me about it.)*
5. ใครช่วยดูเรื่องยาให้? *(Who helps you with your medicines?)*
6. ถ้ามีแอปที่ส่องซองยาแล้วอ่านออกเสียงให้ฟัง จะใช้ไหม? *(If an app read the envelope aloud, would you use it?)*

Ask the helper (son, daughter, or grandchild) too: "How often do you worry about their medicines?"

   3.2 Rules
- Ask permission to quote them. Use no real names or real photos of their envelopes in the app or deck (PDPA).
  You can *describe* them: "a 72-year-old grandmother in Nonthaburi".
- Write down exact words. One real quote goes on slide 2.

   3.3 Your impact test (gives you REAL numbers for the pitch)
On Day 2 evening, with 2–5 people (older relatives, teachers, or even classmates wearing blurry reading glasses as a rough stand-in):
1. Give them 3 fake envelopes. Time how long until they can say *"what, how many, and when"* for all 3. Count the mistakes.
2. Then do the same with the app.
3. Put it on a slide: *"Without the app: 2 min 10 s and 2 mistakes. With ยาใจ: 40 s and 0 mistakes (5 testers)."*

Use your real numbers, even if they're small. "We tested with 5 people" is worth more than any claim.

== 4. The solution: what the app does

   4.1 Core flow (the one thing that must work perfectly)
```
[Open app] → big button "📷 สแกนซองยา" → take photo of envelope
     → Vision reads the text (on-device)
     → label reader turns it into: drug, amount, times, meal timing, warnings
     → BIG result card  +  🔊 reads it aloud in Thai
     → "เพิ่มในยาของฉัน" (Add to my medicines)
     → Today screen: ☀️ เช้า / 🌤 กลางวัน / 🌆 เย็น / 🌙 ก่อนนอน, tick ✓ when taken
```

   4.2 Screens (4 total)

Screen 1 · Home / Today (ContentView)
- Top: today's date in Thai, plus "ถึงเวลาทานยา: เช้า" ("time for your morning medicines") based on the clock.
- Middle: medicines grouped by time slot, each a big row with a ✓ button.
- Bottom: a giant "📷 สแกนซองยา" button (minimum 60 pt tall).
- Empty state: "ยังไม่มียา: กดปุ่มด้านล่างเพื่อสแกนซองยา" ("no medicines yet: tap the button below to scan an envelope") with an arrow.

Screen 2 · Scan (ScanView)
- Camera on the iPad. A PhotosPicker fallback (for the simulator, or if the camera is unavailable).
- While reading: "กำลังอ่านซองยา…" ("reading the envelope…") with a progress spinner.

Screen 3 · Result card (ResultCardView)
- 💊 Amlodipine 5 mg, with a friendly name underneath: *ยาลดความดันโลหิต* (blood pressure medicine)
- ครั้งละ 1 เม็ด (1 tablet each time) in very big text
- Time chips: ☀️ เช้า ✅ · 🌆 เย็น ✅ (morning, evening)
- 🍚 หลังอาหาร (after meals)
- ⚠️ warnings, if any
- Buttons: 🔊 อ่านออกเสียง (read aloud) · ✏️ แก้ไข (edit) · ✅ เพิ่มในยาของฉัน (add to my medicines)
- Small, always visible: *"ตรวจกับฉลากจริงทุกครั้ง · ไม่ใช่คำแนะนำทางการแพทย์"* ("always check the real label · not medical advice")
- A small badge showing who read it: "อ่านในเครื่อง (On-device)" or "AI cluster" (required by your AGENTS.md)
- "ดูข้อความที่อ่านได้" (show the scanned text): expands the raw text, so a family member can compare it with the label

Screen 4 · Medicine detail (MedicineDetailView)
- Same as the card, plus delete, plus the "read aloud" button.

   4.3 What's special (say this in the pitch)
1. Works with no internet. Rural areas, a hospital basement, or at home with no Wi-Fi.
2. Private. Medicine info never leaves the iPad.
3. Voice + huge text. Made for older eyes, not for programmers.
4. Zero setup. No login, no typing. Open, scan, done.

== 6. The AI design

   6.1 The honest truth about Thai (real knowledge, very important)
- Apple's on-device model (Foundation Models / AFM): as of iOS 26.1 it supports 16 languages: English, Danish, Dutch, French, German,
  Italian, Norwegian, Portuguese, Spanish, Swedish, Turkish, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese. Thai is not on the list.
  Your iPad runs iPadOS 27, which may add more, but no source confirms Thai yet.
  Developers also report that Foundation Models can be unavailable if the device's system language isn't supported, and Apple's API has an error called
  `unsupportedLanguageOrLocale`.
- Vision text recognition (OCR): the handbook says to pass `"th-TH"`, but I couldn't find an Apple page that confirms Thai is supported.
  The handbook's own `TextReader.swift` quietly drops languages the device doesn't support. So check on the iPad.
- The AI cluster: `.ocr` = Typhoon OCR (made by SCB 10X, built for Thai + English). `.chat` = Gemma / Qwen, which handle Thai well. But it needs event Wi-Fi.

➡️ That's why the design has layers. Each layer has a real job, and the app never depends on just one.

   6.2 🧪 The 30-minute spike test (do it FIRST, tonight, on the iPad)
Make a temporary `SpikeTestView.swift` (code in 7.9) that shows on screen:
1. Does Vision's `supportedRecognitionLanguages` include th?
2. `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability`: is it available?
3. Does `supportsLocale(Locale(identifier: "th_TH"))` return true?
4. Give the model a Thai label text with `@Generable PillInfo`: does it return a correct result, or throw an error?
5. Is there a Thai voice (`AVSpeechSynthesisVoice(language: "th-TH")` not nil)?

   6.3 Decide your plan from the results

| Result | Plan | The pipeline |
|---|---|---|
| Vision Thai ✅, AFM Thai ✅ | Plan A (best story) | Vision → rules + on-device Foundation Models (`@Generable`) → card. 100% on-device |
| Vision Thai ✅, AFM Thai ❌ | Plan B (most likely) | Vision → rules (offline, instant) → AI cluster `.chat` double-checks when on Wi-Fi |
| Vision Thai ❌ | Plan C | AI cluster `.ocr` (Typhoon) reads the text when online. Offline: Vision still reads the English drug name + Latin codes (bid, pc, hs) |
| No Thai voice | — | Download it: iPad Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Spoken Content ▸ Voices ▸ Thai. Or use cluster `.tts` |

   6.4 The pipeline (Plan A/B)
```
 📷 Photo
   │
   ▼
 [Vision RecognizeTextRequest]  ← on-device neural network (Neural Engine), th + en
   │   lines of text
   ▼
 [LabelParser: Thai rules]      ← on-device, instant, never fails
   │   PillInfo (from rules)
   ▼
 [LLM double-check]             ← Plan A: Foundation Models (on-device)
   │                              Plan B: AI cluster .chat (Wi-Fi only)
   │   PillInfo (from the AI) → merged with the rules result
   ▼
 [Result card] + [🔊 AVSpeechSynthesizer th-TH] + badge "who answered"
```

   6.5 Why each tool (say this to judges, about 2 sentences each)
- Vision (on-device): "Reading text is a small job, so it runs on the iPad. It's free, instant, works offline, and the photo never leaves the device."
- Rules for Thai labels: "Thai hospital labels use the same standard phrases: ครั้งละ… วันละ… หลังอาหาร. Simple rules read them perfectly, offline, every time.
  We didn't use a big AI where a small, reliable tool works better."
- LLM double-check: "Real labels can be messy, wrinkled, or worded differently. The language model handles those cases. We [tested Apple's on-device model with Thai: it works / Thai isn't supported yet],
  so we use [on-device Foundation Models / the event cluster], and the app still works without it."
- Voice (on-device): "Many older people find listening easier than reading. iOS has a Thai voice built in, so this works offline too."

  | 🏆 This matches what the kickoff said: *do small jobs on-device, use the cluster only when you must, and a bigger model doesn't mean more points.*

   6.6 Safety design (medicine = be careful)
- The LLM is told: copy only what's printed. Never guess a drug, dose, or time.
- Always show the raw scanned text, so a human can compare.
- If the rules and the AI disagree, show the field in orange with "โปรดตรวจสอบ" ("please check").
- If times were guessed from "วันละ 2 ครั้ง" (twice a day) without saying which times, mark them "เดาจากวันละ 2 ครั้ง" ("guessed from twice a day").
- "ไม่ใช่คำแนะนำทางการแพทย์ · ถามเภสัชกรเมื่อไม่แน่ใจ" ("not medical advice · ask a pharmacist if unsure") appears on every result.
- No medical advice features: no "is this safe to mix?" and no dose changes.

   7.3 Data model: `PillInfo` (shared by rules, AI, and UI)
```swift
// Models/PillInfo.swift
import FoundationModels

@Generable
enum MealTiming {
    case before, after, with, anytime
}

@Generable
struct PillInfo {
    @Guide(description: "Drug name in English exactly as printed, e.g. Amlodipine. Empty if not printed.")
    var drugName: String
    @Guide(description: "Strength exactly as printed, e.g. 5 mg. Empty if not printed.")
    var strength: String
    @Guide(description: "How many units each time, e.g. 1 or 0.5. Use 1 if not printed.")
    var amountPerDose: Double
    @Guide(description: "Unit in Thai as printed: เม็ด, แคปซูล, ช้อนชา, ช้อนโต๊ะ, or ซอง")
    var unit: String
    var morning: Bool
    var noon: Bool
    var evening: Bool
    var bedtime: Bool
    var mealTiming: MealTiming
    @Guide(description: "True only if the label says to take it when needed, e.g. เมื่อมีอาการ")
    var asNeeded: Bool
    @Guide(description: "Warnings printed on the label, copied in Thai, short")
    var warnings: [String]
}

extension PillInfo {
    static let empty = PillInfo(drugName: "", strength: "", amountPerDose: 1, unit: "เม็ด",
                                morning: false, noon: false, evening: false, bedtime: false,
                                mealTiming: .anytime, asNeeded: false, warnings: [])
    var hasAnyTime: Bool { morning || noon || evening || bedtime }
}

extension MealTiming {
    var thai: String {
        switch self {
        case .before: "ก่อนอาหาร"
        case .after: "หลังอาหาร"
        case .with: "พร้อมอาหาร"
        case .anytime: ""
        }
    }
}
```
  | `import FoundationModels` is fine on iOS 26 and builds in both Xcode 26 and 27. On a device without Apple Intelligence, the *type* still works.
  | Only calling the model needs an availability check.

   7.5 The camera
```swift
// Reading/CameraPicker.swift
import SwiftUI
import UIKit

/// Opens the iPad camera and returns one photo. Check
/// `UIImagePickerController.isSourceTypeAvailable(.camera)` before showing it.
struct CameraPicker: UIViewControllerRepresentable {
    @Binding var image: UIImage?
    @Environment(\.dismiss) private var dismiss

    func makeUIViewController(context: Context) -> UIImagePickerController {
        let picker = UIImagePickerController()
        picker.sourceType = .camera
        picker.delegate = context.coordinator
        return picker
    }

    func updateUIViewController(_ picker: UIImagePickerController, context: Context) {}

    func makeCoordinator() -> Coordinator { Coordinator(self) }

    final class Coordinator: NSObject, UIImagePickerControllerDelegate, UINavigationControllerDelegate {
        let parent: CameraPicker
        init(_ parent: CameraPicker) { self.parent = parent }

        func imagePickerController(_ picker: UIImagePickerController,
                                   didFinishPickingMediaWithInfo info: [UIImagePickerController.InfoKey: Any]) {
            parent.image = info[.originalImage] as? UIImage
            parent.dismiss()
        }

        func imagePickerControllerDidCancel(_ picker: UIImagePickerController) {
            parent.dismiss()
        }
    }
}
```
- Required: target ▸ Info ▸ add `Privacy - Camera Usage Description` = `ใช้กล้องเพื่ออ่านฉลากซองยา` ("used to read medicine envelope labels"). Without it, the app crashes when the camera opens.
- Also add a `PhotosPicker` button ("เลือกรูปจากคลังภาพ", choose from photo library). It needs no permission key, works in the simulator (drag a photo onto it),
  and is your backup if the camera misbehaves on stage.
- Photo tips: good light, fill the frame with the sticker, avoid glare on the plastic (tilt slightly).

   7.6 The label reader: `LabelParser` (the heart, Person B)
Thai hospital labels follow a pattern. Real knowledge about the text you'll see:

| On the label | Means | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `Amlodipine 5 mg` | drug + strength | English word + number + mg/mcg/g/ml |
| `ครั้งละ 1 เม็ด` / `ครั้งละ ½ เม็ด` / `ครั้งละ ครึ่ง เม็ด` | amount each time (1 tablet, half a tablet) | number or ครึ่ง/½ after ครั้งละ |
| `วันละ 2 ครั้ง` | 2 times a day | number between วันละ and ครั้ง |
| `เช้า` `กลางวัน`/`เที่ยง` `เย็น` `ก่อนนอน` | morning, noon, evening, bedtime | keyword |
| `ก่อนอาหาร` (often `30 นาที`) / `หลังอาหาร` / `หลังอาหารทันที` / `พร้อมอาหาร` | before / after / right after / with meals | keyword |
| `เมื่อมีอาการ` `เวลามีอาการ` `เมื่อปวด` `เมื่อมีไข้` `ทุก 4-6 ชั่วโมง` | when needed (when symptoms appear, when in pain, when feverish, every 4–6 hours) | → asNeeded |
| `ac` `pc` `hs` `od` `bid` `tid` `qid` `prn` | Latin: before meals, after meals, bedtime, once a day, 2×, 3×, 4× a day, when needed | Latin codes (doctors' shorthand) |
| `อาจทำให้ง่วงซึม` `ห้าม…` `ควร…` `ทานจนหมด` `ดื่มน้ำมากๆ` | may cause drowsiness, do not…, should…, finish the whole course, drink lots of water | → warnings |
| `๑ ๒ ๓` | Thai digits | convert to 1 2 3 |

Thai OCR gotchas (real):
- Thai digits ๐–๙ → convert to 0–9.
- สระอำ (ำ) sometimes comes out as two characters, nikhahit (ํ) + sara aa (า). Join them back.
- OCR sometimes adds spaces between Thai letters. Search in a copy with all spaces removed.
- Thai tone marks (่ ้ ๊ ๋) combine with the letter before them into one Swift `Character`.
  If you ever need to strip them, work on `unicodeScalars`, not Characters.

```swift
// Understanding/LabelParser.swift
import Foundation

/// Reads a Thai medicine label with fixed rules. Offline, instant, and the same answer every time.
enum LabelParser {

    static func parse(_ lines: [String]) -> PillInfo {
        let text = normalize(lines.joined(separator: "\n"))
        let compact = text.replacingOccurrences(of: " ", with: "")      // OCR adds random spaces in Thai
        let latin = text.lowercased().replacingOccurrences(of: ".", with: "") // "b.i.d." → "bid"
        var info = PillInfo.empty

        // 1. Drug + strength: "Amlodipine 5 mg"
        if let m = text.firstMatch(of: /([A-Za-z][A-Za-z\-]{3,})\s*(\d+(?:\.\d+)?)\s*(mg|mcg|g|ml)\b/.ignoresCase()) {
            info.drugName = String(m.1).capitalized
            info.strength = "\(m.2) \(m.3.lowercased())"
        }

        // 2. Amount each time: "ครั้งละ1เม็ด", "ครั้งละครึ่งเม็ด", "ครั้งละ½เม็ด"
        if let m = compact.firstMatch(of: /ครั้งละ(\d+(?:\.\d+)?|ครึ่ง|½)(เม็ด|แคปซูล|ช้อนชา|ช้อนโต๊ะ|ซอง)?/) {
            info.amountPerDose = (m.1 == "ครึ่ง" || m.1 == "½") ? 0.5 : (Double(m.1) ?? 1)
            if let unit = m.2 { info.unit = String(unit) }
        }

        // 3. Times of day
        info.morning = compact.contains("เช้า")
        info.noon    = compact.contains("กลางวัน") || compact.contains("เที่ยง")
        info.evening = compact.contains("เย็น")
        info.bedtime = compact.contains("ก่อนนอน") || latin.contains(/\bhs\b/)

        // 4. When needed
        let asNeededWords = ["เมื่อมีอาการ", "เวลามีอาการ", "เมื่อปวด", "เมื่อมีไข้", "ทุก4-6ชั่วโมง"]
        info.asNeeded = asNeededWords.contains { compact.contains($0) } || latin.contains(/\bprn\b/)

        // 5. Times per day → fill in times only if the label didn't name them
        var timesPerDay = 0
        if let m = compact.firstMatch(of: /วันละ(\d)ครั้ง/) { timesPerDay = Int(m.1) ?? 0 }
        if latin.contains(/\bod\b/)  { timesPerDay = 1 }
        if latin.contains(/\bbid\b/) { timesPerDay = 2 }
        if latin.contains(/\btid\b/) { timesPerDay = 3 }
        if latin.contains(/\bqid\b/) { timesPerDay = 4 }
        if !info.hasAnyTime && !info.asNeeded {
            // These are a GUESS. The card must say "please check".
            switch timesPerDay {
            case 1: info.morning = true
            case 2: info.morning = true; info.evening = true
            case 3: info.morning = true; info.noon = true; info.evening = true
            case 4: info.morning = true; info.noon = true; info.evening = true; info.bedtime = true
            default: break
            }
        }

        // 6. Meals
        if compact.contains("ก่อนอาหาร") || latin.contains(/\bac\b/) {
            info.mealTiming = .before
        } else if compact.contains("หลังอาหาร") || latin.contains(/\bpc\b/) {
            info.mealTiming = .after
        } else if compact.contains("พร้อมอาหาร") {
            info.mealTiming = .with
        }

        // 7. Warnings: keep the whole line so nothing gets lost
        let warningWords = ["ง่วง", "ห้าม", "ระวัง", "ไม่ควร", "จนหมด", "ดื่มน้ำ"]
        info.warnings = lines.filter { line in warningWords.contains { line.contains($0) } }
                             .map { $0.trimmingCharacters(in: .whitespaces) }
        return info
    }

    /// Thai digits → 0-9 and fix สระอำ written as two characters.
    static func normalize(_ s: String) -> String {
        var out = String.UnicodeScalarView()
        for u in s.unicodeScalars {
            if (0x0E50...0x0E59).contains(u.value), let digit = Unicode.Scalar(u.value - 0x0E50 + 0x30) {
                out.append(digit)
            } else {
                out.append(u)
            }
        }
        return String(out).replacingOccurrences(of: "\u{0E4D}\u{0E32}", with: "\u{0E33}")
    }
}
```

// Understanding/PillExtractor.swift
import FoundationModels

enum AnswerSource: String {
    case rules    = "อ่านในเครื่อง"       // On-device rules
    case onDevice = "AI ในเครื่อง"        // On-device Foundation Models
    case cluster  = "AI cluster"
}

enum PillExtractor {
    static let instructions = """
    You read Thai medicine envelope labels. Copy facts exactly as printed.
    Never guess a drug, dose, or time that is not printed. If something is missing, leave it empty or false.
    """

    /// Rules always run. An LLM improves the result when one is available.
    static func extract(from lines: [String]) async -> (PillInfo, AnswerSource) {
        let fromRules = LabelParser.parse(lines)
        let text = lines.joined(separator: "\n")

        // Plan A: on-device Foundation Models (only if the spike test said Thai works)
        if let ai = try? await askOnDevice(text) {
            return (merge(ai: ai, rules: fromRules), .onDevice)
        }
        // Plan B: the AI cluster (uncomment after adding the HackathonAI package + AICluster.swift)
        // if let ai = try? await askCluster(text) {
        //     return (merge(ai: ai, rules: fromRules), .cluster)
        // }
        return (fromRules, .rules)
    }

    static func askOnDevice(_ text: String) async throws -> PillInfo? {
        let model = SystemLanguageModel.default
        guard case .available = model.availability,
              model.supportsLocale(Locale(identifier: "th_TH")) else { return nil }
        let session = LanguageModelSession(instructions: instructions)
        return try await session.respond(to: text, generating: PillInfo.self).content
    }

    /// Prefer the AI's answer, but keep anything the AI left empty from the rules.
    static func merge(ai: PillInfo, rules: PillInfo) -> PillInfo {
        var result = ai
        if result.drugName.isEmpty { result.drugName = rules.drugName }
        if result.strength.isEmpty { result.strength = rules.strength }
        if !result.hasAnyTime && !result.asNeeded {
            result.morning = rules.morning; result.noon = rules.noon
            result.evening = rules.evening; result.bedtime = rules.bedtime
        }
        if result.warnings.isEmpty { result.warnings = rules.warnings }
        return result
    }
}

// Speaking/SpeechReader.swift
import AVFoundation

/// Reads Thai text aloud using the iPad's built-in Thai voice. Works offline.
@MainActor
final class SpeechReader {
    private let synthesizer = AVSpeechSynthesizer()   // must be kept alive, or speech stops instantly

    func speak(_ text: String) {
        try? AVAudioSession.sharedInstance().setCategory(.playback)   // play even if the iPhone is on silent
        synthesizer.stopSpeaking(at: .immediate)
        let utterance = AVSpeechUtterance(string: text)
        utterance.voice = AVSpeechSynthesisVoice(language: "th-TH")
        utterance.rate = 0.42          // a little slower than normal (0.5) for older listeners
        synthesizer.speak(utterance)
    }
}

extension PillInfo {
    /// e.g. "ยา Amlodipine. ยาลดความดันโลหิต. กินครั้งละ 1 เม็ด. ตอนเช้า และตอนเย็น. หลังอาหาร."
    var spokenThai: String {
        var times: [String] = []
        if morning { times.append("ตอนเช้า") }
        if noon { times.append("ตอนกลางวัน") }
        if evening { times.append("ตอนเย็น") }
        if bedtime { times.append("ก่อนนอน") }
        let amount = amountPerDose == 0.5 ? "ครึ่ง" : String(format: "%g", amountPerDose)
        var parts = ["ยา \(drugName)"]
        if let friendly = DrugDictionary.friendlyName(for: drugName) { parts.append(friendly) }
        parts.append("กินครั้งละ \(amount) \(unit)")
        parts.append(asNeeded ? "เมื่อมีอาการ" : times.joined(separator: " และ"))
        if !mealTiming.thai.isEmpty { parts.append(mealTiming.thai) }
        parts += warnings
        return parts.joined(separator: ". ")
    }
}

== 11. Design guide

Made for older eyes and shaky hands, following Apple's Human Interface Guidelines.
- Text: use Dynamic Type styles (`.largeTitle`, `.title`, `.title2`), never fixed sizes. Make "ครั้งละ 1 เม็ด" (1 tablet each time) the biggest thing on the card.
  Test at the largest accessibility size.
- Buttons: at least 60 pt tall (Apple's minimum is 44 pt; go bigger for older users). Full-width. `.borderedProminent` for the main action.
- Colors: system colors only (`.primary`, `.secondary`, `.orange` for warnings, `.green` for taken ✓, `.accentColor`). They work in dark mode automatically.
  Never use color as the only signal: add an icon and words.
- SF Symbols: `pills.fill`, `camera.fill`, `speaker.wave.2.fill`, `sun.max.fill`, `sun.min.fill`, `sunset.fill`, `moon.stars.fill`,
  `fork.knife`, `exclamationmark.triangle.fill`, `checkmark.circle.fill`, `pencil`.
- Language: Thai UI. Short, friendly words: "กินยา" or "ทานยา" (take medicine), not technical terms.
- One thing per screen. No tabs needed: Today → Scan → Card.
- Feedback: a light haptic when ✓ is tapped (`.sensoryFeedback(.success, trigger:)`).
- Accessibility: `.accessibilityLabel("อ่านออกเสียง")` ("read aloud") on icon buttons. Test once with VoiceOver.
- Mentor clinic: bring the iPad with the real flow and ask: *"Would my grandma understand this screen in 3 seconds?"*

== 16. Real knowledge to know

   16.1 Words everyone on the team must be able to explain
- OCR (Optical Character Recognition): turning a photo of text into real text. Apple's Vision framework does it with a neural network on the device.
- Neural Engine: the AI chip inside the iPad's M-series processor. It runs models fast and privately.
- LLM (Large Language Model): an AI that understands and writes language, like ChatGPT. Apple Foundation Models (AFM) is a small one that runs *on* the iPad.
- `@Generable`: a Swift feature that makes the LLM answer in *your* struct's exact shape (e.g. `PillInfo`), instead of loose text. More reliable than asking for JSON.
- Rules / parser: normal code that looks for patterns like "ครั้งละ 1 เม็ด". Fast, predictable, offline.
- On-device vs cloud vs cluster: on-device = on the iPad (private, offline, free). Cloud = a company's servers (needs internet, data leaves).
  The cluster = the organizers' Macs at the event (more powerful, event Wi-Fi only).
- Fallback: a backup path when the first choice fails (e.g. no Wi-Fi → use on-device).
- PDPA: Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act (B.E. 2562, enforced 2022). Health information is *sensitive data*. That's why we only use fake data.
- Polypharmacy: taking 5+ medicines. Common in older people, and it raises the risk of mistakes.
- Presbyopia (สายตายาวตามอายุ): age-related difficulty seeing small things up close, starting around 40–45.

   16.2 How a Thai medicine envelope is laid out (usually)
1. Hospital or clinic name + phone number
2. Patient name + HN (hospital number) + date
3. Drug name (English generic name) + strength (e.g. Metformin 500 mg)
4. Sometimes the Thai purpose: "ยาเบาหวาน" (diabetes medicine)
5. How to take it: รับประทานครั้งละ… วันละ… ครั้ง… ก่อน/หลังอาหาร… เช้า/กลางวัน/เย็น/ก่อนนอน (take … each time, … times a day, before/after meals, morning/noon/evening/bedtime)
6. Warnings (คำเตือน): drowsiness, finish the course, avoid alcohol, etc.
7. Quantity + expiry / date dispensed

Labels differ between hospitals, so look at a few real ones at home (don't photograph patient names for the app or deck).

   16.3 Latin dosing abbreviations (you'll see them on some labels)
| Code | Latin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| od / qd | omne in die / quaque die | once a day |
| bid | bis in die | twice a day |
| tid | ter in die | 3 times a day |
| qid | quater in die | 4 times a day |
| ac | ante cibum | before meals |
| pc | post cibum | after meals |
| hs | hora somni | at bedtime |
| prn | pro re nata | when needed |

   16.4 Apple tech facts to get right
- Foundation Models needs: an Apple Intelligence-capable device with Apple Intelligence turned on. Check `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability`
  (`.available` or `.unavailable(reason)`: device not eligible, Apple Intelligence not enabled, model not ready). The Intel Mac simulator can't run it.
- On-device model languages (iOS 26.1): 16 languages, no Thai. iPadOS 27 is unknown, so test it.
- Vision `RecognizeTextRequest` (the Swift API, iOS 18+): `.accurate` level, set `recognitionLanguages`, read `observation.transcript`.
- AVSpeechSynthesizer has a built-in Thai voice (`th-TH`). It works offline. More voices: Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Spoken Content.
- SwiftData (`@Model`, `@Query`, `.modelContainer`) saves data on the device. It's iOS 17+, so fine for you.
- Camera = real iPad only. The simulator has no camera, so use PhotosPicker there.

   16.5 Why "a smaller tool can score higher"
The kickoff literally said a team using Foundation Models *could score higher* than one using MLX, depending on the use.
Judges reward "you understood the problem and chose sensibly", not "you used the biggest model". Your rules + on-device + optional LLM design is exactly that.
